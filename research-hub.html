Television

LG 77″ B6 · verdict: keep, time the buy

The B6 keeps the entire gaming stack of the dearer LGs — four 48 Gbps HDMI 2.1 ports, 4K120/144 VRR, ~9 ms lag — and its one real weakness, HDR peak brightness, is the thing a blacked-out room hides best. 9,499 is fair (US street parity); 8,799 is the tracked floor. The wildcards: eXtra's 2025 closeouts — 77″ C5 at 7,599 and 77″ G5 (flagship panel) at 9,799 — both out of stock but worth a restock alert.

  • Gaming stack is B-series-complete: four full-bandwidth HDMI 2.1, 144 Hz VRR, G-Sync/FreeSync, ALLM, HGiG. The 165 Hz on C6/G6 is irrelevant on PS5 and marginal on PC.
  • Input lag ~8.9 ms (boost mode), ~5 ms at 120 Hz — effectively identical in feel to the C6.
  • Burn-in reality per RTINGS' 10,000-hour program: mixed gaming + video rarely damages panels; the risk case is static desktop UI. Auto-hide the taskbar, dark mode, short screen-off timer — and the black room lets you run lower OLED light, which cuts wear further.
  • Tom's Guide measured the B6's color accuracy beating the C6's and published a piece arguing against overspending on the C6.
  • Import makes no sense: US street = KSA price before shipping, VAT, customs, and lost local warranty.
Expert reviews
  • Tom's Guide · 4.5/5B6 review — ~780 nits HDR, 8.9 ms lag, full gaming feature set; rated one of 2026's best gaming TVs, with color accuracy that edged the C6 in their lab.
  • Tom's GuideC6 vs B6 — most buyers shouldn't pay the C6 premium; it wins mainly on brightness.
  • TechRadar · 4.5/5B6 review — excellent gaming-and-movies OLED, brightest B-series yet.

Receiver

Denon X2800H · verdict: keep · one lane choice

95 W into 92 dB-sensitive Klipsch in a sealed 20 m² room is more headroom than it sounds — and because both sources feed the TV, the AVR's HDMI board only ever does eARC duty, sidestepping most gaming-passthrough pain. The honest reasons to pay ~2,950 more for the X3800H are Audyssey XT32 and independent dual-sub outputs; with one sub in a treated room, the X2800H wins. One decision remains: local stock at 5,938 with KSA warranty, or the identical EU-variant at 3,865 with EU-handled warranty — the safest lane and the cheapest lane point in opposite directions, and a US law firm's investigation into Denon HDMI-board failures says pick the lane consciously.

  • All 7 channels consumed by 5.1.2 — buy it knowing the ceiling is 5.1.2. Any future 5.1.4 or aligned dual subs means the X3800H instead.
  • The two sub outputs are a Y-split (same signal) — fine with one SB-1000 Pro, useless for aligning a second. If dual subs are realistically coming, the AVR pick reopens.
  • eARC insurance stack: day-one firmware on TV and AVR, disable the ARC toggle when using eARC, switch PS5 to bitstream if dropouts appear, keep one fiber run re-routable to the AVR as a PS5 rescue lane (its 40 Gbps inputs can't carry the PC's 4K144).
  • Runs hot: a sealed credenza is the documented failure scenario. Vented back panel or a quiet AC Infinity fan.
Expert reviews & community

Speakers · front stage & surrounds

Klipsch RP · verdict: keep, lock in now

Keep the RP-500M II + RP-500C II + R-41M basket. At 1,949 for a locally stocked pair, the Klipsch undercuts every credible rival in KSA while being the only option with an exactly timbre-matched centre. Measurements show gen-II is far more neutral than Klipsch's bright reputation — and your heavily damped black room absorbs what little top-end lift remains. Community A/Bs vs KEF/ELAC repeatedly land on: Klipsch for slam and cinema dynamics, the others for refinement — this is a cinema room. Stock is the risk: 4 pairs of fronts left at the deal price.

  • Erin's Audio Corner: gen-II is one of the most neutral speakers Klipsch has made; the only caveat is a mild 10–15 kHz lift — aim the speakers straight ahead (listen 20–30° off-axis) and it tilts flat.
  • The horn's controlled directivity throws less energy at side walls — fewer early reflections, a genuinely good match for a treated room.
  • Centre is the dialogue channel: RP-500C II shares the exact tweeter, horn, and woofers with the fronts. It loses clarity past ~±10–15° off-axis — fine for a centered sofa, another reason to settle the layout.
  • Placement: rear ports need 15–20 cm off the wall — plan proper stands flanking the TV, not shelf mounting.
  • If it ever sounds hot: run Audyssey, straighten the toe-in, trim treble 1–2 dB — in that order. Don't buy different speakers over a solvable shelf.

Atmos heights

corrected pick: Polk RC80i · install rules matter most

The KEF price collapsed the original plan — Ci160QR is ~2,944 SAR each, not 1,700 a pair. New default: Polk RC80i at 1,094/pair with the aimable tweeters pointed at the seat, or KEF Ci160.2CR at 1,963 each as the Uni-Q middle path. Whatever goes in: seal the cutouts. An open speaker hole is the biggest leak in your soundproofed ceiling — back-box or intumescent fire hood, sealed with acoustic caulk, mineral wool kept loose behind the driver. And since the ceiling is fresh gypsum, cabling now is nearly free — which makes “skip but pre-wire” the worst-case floor, never “no cables.”

  • Placement: with a ~2.7–3 m ceiling and ears at 1.1 m, Top Front wants the pair ~1.1–1.6 m ahead of the seat, in line with the front L/R — calculator. One pair cannot serve both the sofa and the desk: the main listening position must be decided before the ceiling is boarded.
  • Coordinate cutouts (196 mm for KEF) with the recessed-light layout and void depth — and order rough-in frames before boarding.
  • Upfiring modules are broadly dismissed by the community as not producing the overhead effect — ceiling speakers or nothing.
  • Do not repurpose R-41M bookshelves as heights — rear-ported, no mount; Klipsch's own answer is the CDT in-ceiling line (voice-matched, pivoting tweeter) if staying in-family.

Subwoofer

SVS SB-1000 Pro · verdict: keep · new price & lane

A sealed 20–24 m² room is exactly where a small sealed sub punches above its weight — room gain props up the sealed rolloff and pulls usable extension to 20 Hz at your listening levels. Audioholics measured ~101 dB at 31.5 Hz from a 33 cm cube, and the app's parametric EQ is unmatched in the class. The correction: noon's lane is dead; budget 3,758–3,816 via amazon.sa — and because those listings ship from the US, confirm the unit is 230 V with the seller before paying. Skip the SB-2000 Pro; the community-preferred upgrade in small rooms is a second SB-1000 Pro later (which would also reopen the AVR question — the X2800H can't align a pair).

  • Sealed vs ported: tighter, punchier, easier to place — the gap to ported shrinks in a small sealed room until the sealed box's speed wins.
  • Hollow gypsum rattles: isolation feet under the sub, then a listen-and-tighten pass on the door, panels, and light trims after calibration.
  • Calibration order: Audyssey first with sub gain at mid, then the SVS app's PEQ for the one or two peaks it leaves. Don't stack both blind.

Acoustics

quotes out NOW · the only unpriced line

The isolation that keeps sound in also keeps bass energy in — a sealed room booms worse than a leaky one, so treatment is not optional. The prescription from GIK's guidance: ~20–25% wall coverage (8–12 panels of 60×120 cm, 10 cm mineral wool), floor-to-ceiling corner traps (front two corners minimum), thick rug + felt underlay, and ceiling panels around — never over — the Atmos speakers. Black fabric is a stocked industry standard, and in this room dark panels double as light-reflection control. Every KSA supplier is quote-based with fabrication lead time: this is the December long pole, and quotes go out now.

  • The mirror trick finds panel spots: helper slides a mirror along the wall; wherever a speaker appears from the seat is a reflection zone. Side walls and ceiling first, then back wall.
  • The slat wall is finish, not treatment: felt-backed slats absorb only mids/highs (NRC 0.6–0.9 above 250 Hz, nothing below). Perfect on the back wall where diffusion prevents over-deadening — and a walnut slat wall is also the anti-cave design move.
  • Amazon foam is decoration: thin wedge foam and 9 mm PET tiles do nothing below 500 Hz. Real spec: rigid mineral wool 60–100 kg/m³, 5–10 cm.
  • DIY route exists: Saudi Rock Wool Factory slabs + wood frames + black breathable fabric = ideal spec at a fraction of finished-panel cost.

Cables & signal

RUIPRO fiber · test before the ceiling closes

At 15 m, copper HDMI 2.1 is physically impossible — certification tops out around 3 m. Both runs must be hybrid fiber (AOC). The forum-consensus pick at exactly this length and use case is RUIPRO, 333 SAR on amazon.sa (5-year warranty); the ATC-certified belt-and-braces option is Zeskit at 679. The two habits that eliminate almost every AOC horror story: respect the Source/Display direction labels, and soak-test each run at 4K120 10-bit HDR VRR for hours from both the PC and PS5 before anything closes up.

  • Never break the run: no wall plates, no couplers, no adapters — one unbroken cable from source to TV.
  • Pull by the body, never the head — the optics live in the connector, and a kinked fiber is dead forever. Decide direction before pulling: Source end starts at the desk.
  • Split-the-risk option: certified Zeskit for the fussier PC run, RUIPRO for the PS5 — ~1,013 total.
  • USB-to-sofa: proper active extenders add under 1 ms (gaming-safe); the 25–45 SAR passive RJ45 baluns on the same search page are USB 1.1 junk. But in a 4–6 m room the dongles likely reach anyway — run a spare Cat6 in the trunking and decide later.

Lighting

two jobs: bias + warm zones

A black room with an OLED needs two different lights. Behind the panel: a neutral 6500 K bias light — OLED's perfect blacks make pupil fatigue worse in a dark room, and bias lighting is the standard fix that also deepens perceived contrast. Around the seating: warm 2700–3000 K dimmable zones, always behind the seating plane so nothing reflects in the glossy panel. The KSA pick is the Govee Envisual T2 75–85″ (~969) — camera-based, so it never touches the 4K120 HDMI chain. The Hue route has two traps here: the KSA-stocked sync box is 4K60-only (it would break your runs), and the local gradient strip is the 65″ size.

  • Zone recipe from batcave builds: cove/perimeter strip + sconces or rear cans over seating (never between seat and screen) + low step lights, all dimmable, scene presets.
  • Black-baffle recessed trims: shiny aluminum rings glare in the OLED. Warm-dim LEDs, IC-rated sealed fixtures in the insulated void.
  • Star ceiling: your hollow void is the easy case for fiber-optic — but it's a construction-phase job (before wallpaper/finish). The budget alternative with 10× the star density: UV-painted mural + hidden black light.
  • Desk: Quntis gives ~80% of the BenQ for a fifth of the price; the Halo 2's rear wall-wash doubles as desk bias light.

The black room

sheen discipline > color choice

Black is the right call — and the ceiling counts as much as the walls. Dark matte surfaces stop screen light re-lighting the image; perceived contrast and the “floating picture” effect improve dramatically. But sheen matters more than shade: matte only, no vinyl gloss, no satin, no metallic — a glossy black wall reflects the screen like a mirror. The anti-cave recipe for a couple's room: pure black held to the TV wall and ceiling zone, deep charcoal textured wallpaper elsewhere, the walnut slat wall as the one warm feature, brass accents, layered warm dimmable light. That combination reads boutique cinema, not cave.

  • The hierarchy: black velvet/flock absorbs light in a different league from flat paint — batcave builders run velvet on the TV wall and first ~1.5 m of ceiling, matte paint or paper beyond.
  • Wallpaper test: view every sample at a low angle under light before committing. Non-woven or fabric-backed textured papers (linen-look, grasscloth) have zero sheen and add slight HF damping; cheap vinyl-faced darks light up like a mirror band.
  • The premium alternative to wallpaper: stretch-fabric track walls (Wallmate-style) — dark acoustically transparent fabric hides the panels, bass traps, and cabling behind wallpaper-crisp seams.
  • Sequencing: velvet/flock, slat wall, in-wall fishing, panel mounts — all before wallpaper hangs.
